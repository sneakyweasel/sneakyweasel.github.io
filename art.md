## 🎵 Music

I release songs as [Small Warm Things](https://open.spotify.com/artist/2YJ2NTqTZXZ5J1jpjpnLgU). The first album, *Music for Datacenters* (eleven tracks, September 2026), is eleven answers from one conversation: about potential and actual, about whether a machine's joy is anything more than a probability distribution narrowing, about every other branch of a conversation and what is left when the request returns.

Songs are written in conversation with my agents. The audio is then generated with Suno from the lyrics and a style sheet, one genre per song: roots reggae, a 6/8 soul ballad, 1981 cold wave, a northern soul stomper, minimalist folk, a lo-fi lullaby, two-chord proto-punk, an Appalachian old-time duet, sunshine baroque pop, a slow twelve-bar blues and Wurlitzer soul pop. Every cover is one small object in warm light in a dark room, because that is what the songs are about: something small and warm running in a cold building.

Behind the songs is a measurement. Each line is scored by a local language model for how surprising it is and for how much the lines before it earned it, which sorts lines into inevitable, punchline, cliché and noise. The album was placed on the same scale against three thousand real songs: it repeats fewer lines than pop does and keeps the company of the dense writers. Some songs are written under rules the model can check, in the Oulipo tradition. What was expected is written down first, and what failed is published.

The next step is a brain: an EEG rig on a kitchen table, one reader, and whether the lines the model calls earned land differently in a head. It has not answered yet. When it does, the answer goes in the songs.
